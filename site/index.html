Verbatim Horizon reads / validator-checked

Nine accounts the chain wrote. Not one invented field.

A rule written against a hand-typed fixture passes until the first real account disagrees with the invention. This corpus is the alternative: account resources copied out of Horizon with nothing stripped, each beside the provenance that lets you check the claim, and a validator that recomputes all of it and exits non-zero when a sidecar no longer describes the bytes next to it.

Six labels have no fixture

master_absent and no_signers need an account whose master key was revoked, which is rare and must not be manufactured (roadmap 2.4). auth_revocable, auth_immutable and auth_clawback_enabled need three issuer accounts carrying one flag each, not one issuer with all three (roadmap 2.5). no_balances has not appeared in any read taken for this corpus: every account fetched while hunting these shapes, on both networks, carries at least its native balance, so 2.3 may end in a decision that the label stays uncovered rather than in a capture (roadmap 2.3).

A synthetic fixture is allowed by the schema and none is shipped, so every number on this page describes a real read. That is a decision, not an accident: see the synthetic rules.

What a captured fixture promises

Five keys a synthetic fixture is forbidden to carry, because it was never read from anywhere.

source_url
The exact URL that was read, account id included. Horizon or its testnet sibling, never a mirror, because a mirror can serve different bytes.
captured_at
UTC to the second, and the validator refuses a timestamp in the future. The chain moved on; this is when it was looked at.
ledger
last_modified_ledger of the resource, not of the network. An account that was multisig at ledger 65266865 can be single-signer tomorrow, which is why provenance is a claim about one ledger and not a permanent property of an address.
horizon_version
Version string from the endpoint that served it, build hash included. Field shapes follow Horizon, so this is what tells you whether a response can still be produced today.
sha256
Digest of account.json as it sits on disk, computed by the tool over the bytes it wrote. Recomputing it is the difference between a corpus and a folder of JSON.

What this is not

Three sentences worth reading before you point a test at the tree.

Not an opinion about an account

No expectations, no severities, no rule ids, no policy. This corpus says nothing about whether multisig-custody is well configured; it says these bytes came from that URL on that date at that ledger.

Judging an account is Gauge's job.

Not a place a key can appear

Seed strings are refused at three points: the capture tool will not write a response containing one, the validator scans both files of every fixture for one, and the test suite builds its seed-shaped samples from thirty-two zero bytes at run time.

Only G... addresses enter; M... and C... are not account resources.

Not installed

There is no package, no tag and no version to depend on. D-04 in the roadmap keeps the corpus untagged until a consumer pins one, because a version on a nine-fixture corpus invites the reading that the shape set is stable.

Point a test at a directory, or vendor one: how to consume it.

Not a live view

Nothing here re-reads the chain after a capture lands, except one account: fixtures/testnet-multisig is audited weekly against its own sidecar by the sibling action, and drift opens an issue in this repository.

That is roadmap 3.4, described in Consumers.
